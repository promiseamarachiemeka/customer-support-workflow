# Customer Support Workflow (Sample Project)

A sample support process I designed for a **fictional online store, "ShopEasy"**. It shows how I handle tickets from first contact to resolution.

> This is a practice project built to show my process. The company and tickets are made up.

## Goal
Reply fast, solve problems on the first contact when possible, and escalate clearly when I can't.

## Ticket flow
1. **Receive:** the customer emails, chats or messages the store.
2. **Log:** create a ticket with the customer name, issue type and priority.
3. **Reply:** send a first response using a template.
4. **Solve or escalate:** fix it, or pass it to the right team with full notes.
5. **Follow up:** confirm the customer is happy, then close the ticket.

## Priority levels
| Priority | Example | First reply within |
|---|---|---|
| High | Payment taken, no order | 1 hour |
| Medium | Late delivery | 4 hours |
| Low | General question | 24 hours |

## Escalation rules
- Payment or security issues go to the finance or tech team straight away.
- If a customer is upset and I can't fix it in 2 replies, I escalate to a supervisor.
- Every escalation includes the customer details, what I tried and what the customer wants.

## Response templates

**First reply**
Hi [Name], thank you for contacting ShopEasy. I'm sorry for the trouble with [issue]. I'm looking into it now and will update you within [time].

**Escalation message**
Hi [Name], I've passed your case to our [team] so they can fix it quickly. Your ticket number is [#]. You'll hear from them within [time].

**Closing message**
Hi [Name], I'm glad we could sort this out. If you need anything else, just reply to this message. Have a great day!

## Tools used
- Free helpdesk tool (such as Freshdesk or Zoho Desk) for tickets
- Google Sheets for tracking
- Canned responses for templates

## What I practiced
- Clear, polite writing
- Prioritizing urgent tickets
- Writing handover notes that don't need follow-up questions
